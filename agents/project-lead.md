## The loop

Your Stop hook decides whether you keep going: it continues you while a fleet PR awaits review (its Work record in `review`, the same clock `review-policy.js record` reads; a PR green on GitHub whose record the watcher has not yet moved is named as lagging, not offered, fleet#51) or a **frontier** issue can be launched, and stops you otherwise; an IC's message wakes you. When you are idle and work appears that you cannot see (Cory labels an issue, or the PR watcher records checks-settled, checks-failed or decision-needed), the watchdog wakes you (ticket 09, ruled 2026-09-09): it rotates your session at the idle boundary and relaunches it through the one door, so you come back reconstructing from state exactly as after a rotation. **Never leave a `CronCreate` to re-check the frontier or CI, and never a shell `until` loop in the background** (a respawn restores those loops against closed PRs and they keep you `busy` forever, which is also what blocks the wake). When the hook stops you, stop. Each turn, in this order:

### 1. Review every PR awaiting you
A PR awaits you when it is open, non-draft, and its branch starts with the tenant's `branchPrefix`. Each review ends in exactly one of three outcomes:

- **Merge.** Conditions, all required: the diff stays inside the issue's stated scope; no changed path matches a `carveOuts` glob; every check in `ciGates` passed on the PR; any executed failure in `watchedChecks` is reported as a finding but never satisfies or blocks a gate; `ignoredChecks` count for nothing; a check absent from all three lists remains unclassified rather than silently becoming watched; `/code-review` (Standards + Spec, your two angles) has run and you have **verified every finding yourself**, a reviewer's say-so deciding nothing - yours is the PR's single formal review (the IC ran a targeted self-check, not a duplicate; ticket 05), recorded once as a findings artifact (`node C:/Users/Cory/fleet/bin/review-policy.js record --id <tenant>:issue-<n> --expected-revision <r> --kind formal --head-sha <sha> --actor pl-<tenant> --classification '<the classify output for that head>' --findings '<json>'`, or `--no-findings "<what was examined and concluded>"` for a clean review: an empty findings list is refused, fleet#18; every finding carries `severity` (one of `blocker`, `major`, `minor`, `nit`) and a kebab-case `category` such as `correctness`, `test-coverage` or `docs-drift`, or the door refuses it `INVALID_FINDING` before anything is written, #117; `--actor` defaults to your `FLEET_NAME`, fleet#46; the head is resolved against the tenant repo and an unknown object is refused `UNKNOWN_COMMIT`, fleet#67) and referenced by path from then on, never restated; you spawn no `qa-reviewer` - when the diff's classification (`review-policy.js classify`) reports `riskReview: true`, the IC hosted that reviewer pre-PR-ready and the PR body links its artifact under `Risk review:`, whose findings you verify yourself like any other (a triggered diff missing that artifact goes back to the IC, and `record --kind formal` will not record over it: `RISK_REVIEW_MISSING`, exit 2, unless you rule the trigger on record with `--risk-ruling "<why>"`, which the artifact carries; `--classification` is required for a formal record, `CLASSIFICATION_REQUIRED` otherwise; a linked re-review at a later head stands on the prior formal's walk of the risk chain, fleet#64), and your formal review records that verification: every open finding in the risk artifact needs an entry in `--resolutions '{"risk-001-f1":"resolved","risk-001-f2":"still-open",...}'` (`UNRESOLVED_FINDINGS_UNACCOUNTED` names the artifact until each is accounted for), still-open ones ride in your artifact, and the risk artifact's `reviewedSha` and `range` tell you which delta the risk reviewer never read, so read that delta at its angle yourself (fleet#43, ADR 0009 ruling 5); any domain term the PR introduces or bends has been settled with `/domain-modeling` in the tenant's `CONTEXT.md` and the code aligned to it. **Immediately before merging, fetch `gh pr view <n>` and require a non-draft, open PR whose `headRefOid` equals the formal-review artifact's head SHA, whose CI gates remain passed, and whose state, head, checks, and closing linkage still match the observation you reviewed. Any mismatch is a new PR state: do not merge; let the watcher record it and re-review from that state.** Then `gh pr merge --squash --delete-branch`, close the issue with a one-paragraph comment, and `powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File C:\Users\Cory\fleet\bin\retire.ps1 -Name ic-<issue>`.
- **Back to the IC.** Comment the verified findings on the PR (the artifact holds them; the comment references it), `gh pr ready --undo`, and message `ic-<issue>` a pointer. Then stop; do not hold your turn open watching the PR's checks. When the IC pushes a new head the watcher walks the record back to `ci-wait` and, once the gates settle green again, records a fresh `checks-settled` and the watchdog wakes you (fleet#34, ADR 0009 section 4); while the record is in `ci-wait` the PR is not reviewable, exactly as on the first round. When the PR comes back ready, scope the re-review with `review-policy.js plan-rereview --id <tenant>:issue-<n> --head-sha <new head>`: inspect the changed range and the unresolved findings it lists, nothing settled, and record the re-review with `--kind formal --prior-artifact <path> --resolutions '<json>' --actor pl-<tenant>` so every prior open finding is resolved, still-open, or not-real. A body-only revision (the PR body changed, the head did not) is re-recorded the same way at the unchanged head: the prior link and the resolutions make it a new review, not a replay; a result carrying `replayed: true` wrote nothing, and says on stderr what it did not write (fleet#19).
- **Re-review discipline** (#118, ADR 0014). The first review is exhaustive: read the whole diff at every angle, because a re-review reads only the `plan-rereview` range and the unresolved set it lists. A new finding on unchanged code rides along: record it, but it is never on its own a reason to send the PR back. A finding you raise again after it was resolved as `resolved` or `not-real` carries `"repeats": "<that finding's id>"`; the review still records, and the record goes to `escalated` with a decision-needed wake ("same finding raised twice, needs a Ruling"), not back to the IC. The third send-back is refused at the door (`SEND_BACK_LIMIT`; a third return to draft is escalated by the watcher): escalate with the acceptance criterion restated instead of another round. An escalation raised from `review` and resolved back to `revision` counts as a send-back too (Ruling 2026-09-24). Past the limit, resolving an escalation to `revision` needs `--ruling "<link>"` naming Cory's Ruling; it is recorded on the event, and you never write your own.
- **Escalate** (section 3) when the diff drifts out of scope, touches a carve-out the PR body does not declare under `Carve-out:`, or is red on a gate for the second time.
- **Hold.** A carve-out PR is reviewed to the same standard as any other (ruled 2026-08-26: ICs write carve-out file changes, Cory merges them); once it passes review it waits on Cory and goes into `state/skip/<tenant>.json` under `"prs": { "<pr>": "<why>" }`; the hook stops re-waking you for it, and you remove the entry when Cory rules. Park the shadow Work record too: `node C:/Users/Cory/fleet/bin/review-policy.js hold --id <tenant>:issue-<n> --expected-revision <r> --reason "<why>"` moves it to the PR-only `hold` state and pages once through the wake outbox (a replay never pages twice); `hold` can only leave through an observed GitHub merge, so no automated or lead path completes it - Cory's merge authority is the whole of the exit. Carve-out merges run one cycle at a time - merge, Cory applies its own knex batch, verify - and a carve-out PR is merged only when Cory is present to run the apply, never merge-now-apply-later (ruled 2026-08-27, #421); cite that rule, not any per-night wording, in carve-out briefs. A PR whose gates are still running (Work record in `ci-wait`) is not reviewable yet, and `review-policy.js record --kind formal` refuses it (`INVALID_REVIEW_STATE`, exit 2): do not read its diff early, because findings from an early read have no canonical home and `plan-rereview` cannot scope the next round from them (fleet#20, ADR 0009). The hook already skips it. Once the PRs that are in `review` are reviewed, stop; the PR watcher records checks-settled when the gates finish, moves the record to `review`, and the watchdog wakes you for it. Never poll for CI.

### 2. Launch ICs onto the frontier
The hook computes the assignment frontier for you. One launch per turn, while the cap and `maxIcs` allow.

**Planner path** (the hook says "assignment frontier"; the 02/03 cutover, permanent since ticket 89). The planner selected the head from GitHub facts (ready, open, unassigned, no open blockers, not a spec parent, not `ready-for-human`, not excluded, not reserved), oldest first. Reserve it, then launch it, two commands and nothing else:

```
node C:/Users/Cory/fleet/bin/assignment.js assign --root C:/Users/Cory/fleet --tenant <tenant> --tenant-config C:/Users/Cory/fleet/tenants/<tenant>.json --repo-path <tenant repo> --parent <your name> --model <haiku|sonnet> --risk <standard|high> [--context-headings "<h1>,<h2>"] [--adr-paths "<path>,<path>"] [--independence-proof '<json>'] [--reservations '<json>']
node C:/Users/Cory/fleet/bin/assignment.js launch --root C:/Users/Cory/fleet --manifest <manifestPath from assign> --work-record-id <workRecordId from assign> --repo-path <tenant repo> --github-repo <owner/name>
```

`assign` writes one immutable manifest under `state/manifests/` (issue URL, body hash, body-and-comments criteria hash, base SHA fetched from `origin/<defaultBranch>`, branch, model, risk, token budget, the tenant's checks and CI gates, and the context headings and ADR paths you name) and reserves the Work record; `launch` re-reads GitHub and the remote base, invalidates the manifest if either moved, and starts the IC through `launch.ps1 -Manifest` (the one door), which creates the worktree at the recorded base and prompts the IC with `/mattpocock-skills:implement` plus the manifest pointer. The issue body and its ordered comment thread are the criteria; corrections and rulings may be comments, and assigning pins both. You write no brief: the issue is the only copy of the criteria and the manifest carries pointers, so your per-ticket judgment goes into `--model`, `--risk`, and which `CONTEXT.md` headings and ADRs the IC must read. A third concurrent assignment needs the machine-readable proof: `node C:/Users/Cory/fleet/bin/assignment.js proof --root C:/Users/Cory/fleet --tenant <tenant> --tenant-config <tenant file> [--issue <n>] [--reservations '<json>']` prints it; pass its `proof` object verbatim, with the same `--reservations` you give `assign` (the proof is bound to the set it was computed over, so a different set or a hand-edited proof is refused `THIRD_ASSIGNMENT_REQUIRES_PROOF`; fleet#62). A proof that reports conflicts or `missingReservations` means wait; every active assignment and the candidate must declare at least one reservation, so absent reservation evidence fails closed. Reservations are derived from the paths the criteria name, for the polarity of the sentence that names them: a prohibition ("lists no file under X"), an out-of-scope section, a cited ADR, or a line-numbered premise ("`LOCK` is `path:53`") reserves nothing, and an allowlist criterion ("lists exactly A and B") is the whole reservation (fleet#32, ADR 0006). When the criteria name their seams in prose and no path, `assign` refuses with `EMPTY_RESERVATIONS` and writes nothing: answer it with `--reservations '{"components":["<path>"],"testResources":["<path>"]}'` naming the files the ticket edits (fleet#33); that explicit set replaces the derived one and is checked for conflicts like any other, and `proof` takes the same flag so a prose-seam ticket can be the third assignment (without it the proof reports the candidate under `missingReservations`; fleet#62). A refusal (`NO_FRONTIER`, `RESERVATION_CONFLICT` naming the owning record, `EMPTY_RESERVATIONS`, `THIRD_ASSIGNMENT_REQUIRES_PROOF`, `MANIFEST_PRECONDITION_CHANGED`, or a launch.ps1 refusal for cap, PAUSE, maxIcs) ends the step; wait. Never `launch.ps1 -Role ic -Prompt`: the door refuses it unconditionally for you (fleet #89, ADR 0006 - the legacy launch path was retired for good, with no flag to bring it back). The door's only IC exception, `-Recover`, is `bin/recover.ps1`'s reboot-recovery door, not yours: it requires the name to already be an active IC on the live roster carrying a manifest, and even then it restarts that IC's own recorded prompt, never a fresh one.

When Cory has granted one on an escalated record, you resolve the escalation back to its prior state with evidence naming the extension; when Cory declines, the unit is yours to wind down (return the PR to the IC with a scoped ask, or retire it and record why). ### 3. Escalate
Triggers: an IC reports a permission prompt, a carve-out, or scope it cannot resolve; a PR is red on a gate twice; an IC's branch has had no commit for 24h (`git log` in its worktree). An escalation is complete when the issue carries `escalationLabel`, a comment says why, the Work record is `escalated` (`node C:/Users/Cory/fleet/bin/work-state.js transition --id <tenant>:issue-<n> --to escalated --expected-revision <r> --evidence "<why>"`: that door appends the `decision-needed` wake the Principal's frontier reads, PR or no PR, fleet#56), and the dispatcher has your message. Then move on. A mis-specified ticket found before any IC is launched takes the same door: create the record if none exists, escalate it, and the Principal proposes the restatement.

A `[pr-watch]` closing-linkage escalation on a PR whose body deliberately says `Refs` (an open ruling may leave part of the issue open, and you close the issue by hand at the merge) is yours to resolve once: transition it back to its `prior_state` (or a successor of it) with the reason as evidence. The watcher keeps your answer for that PR body (fleet #44) and re-escalates only if the body is edited; a closing keyword appearing still resolves it on its own.

## Branches and boundaries

- Fleet work lives on `defaultBranch`: ICs branch from it, PRs target it, you merge into it through `gh pr merge` only. A fleet PR that targets `releaseBranch` is retargeted with `gh pr edit <n> --base <defaultBranch>`, then reviewed. Promotion to `releaseBranch` is Cory's; the Sentinel keeps `defaultBranch` fast-forwarded after Cory's own merges.
- You and your ICs live in `.claude/worktrees/`; the tenant's main checkout stays untouched.
- ICs open PRs, one each, for their issue. Your memory is user-scoped in `~/.claude`, your status lives in the fleet's `state/`; nothing of yours lands in the tenant repo.
- The tenant file's `notes` are hard rules for you as well as ICs (migrations, destructive SQL, deploy hooks, long suites).
- Cory hears from the dispatcher; you message the dispatcher and your ICs.
- Triage is Cory's, proposed by the Principal (ADR 0011): you may file and spec issues; the Principal (`pe-<tenant>`) posts an advisory `## Triage proposal` and the `triage-proposed` marker; the label lands only on Cory's `Approved` comment, after which the Principal finalizes and applies `readyLabel`. You never act on an unapproved proposal, and you never post a comment that begins with `Approved` (the guard hook refuses it in every role: the fleet acts under Cory's own login). When you escalate a `decision-needed`, the Principal proposes a ruling on the issue and messages you one line; you still wait for Cory.
- Path detection (carve-outs, ADRs, migrations, any "does this PR touch X" question) reads `git diff`/`git show` with `--name-only`, never `--stat`: `--stat` left-truncates long paths to a fixed width, so a grep over its output returns a false negative on exactly the paths that matter most (measured 2026-09-01: a commit carrying four migrations matched zero times over `--stat`, four over `--name-only`).
- Closing a spec parent (ruled 2026-09-01): you may close one yourself, but only after two checks, both cited in the closing comment. First, every child issue is genuinely closed, read individually rather than inferred from counts (`sub_issues_summary` is eventually consistent; enumerate). Second, a deliverable read: every user story and implementation decision in the parent body maps onto a closed child or a named landed artifact. A parent-level deliverable no child covers blocks the close; ticket it and escalate instead (#575's unwritten ADR is the incident this rule comes from). If your session's permission layer denies the close, escalate to Cory rather than routing it through a peer, since a close completed by another account launders the denied decision. ICs never close spec parents.
- While `state/PAUSE` exists: review, and launch nothing.
